# Pedagogical design notes

The activities use technology as a shared prompt and pacing surface. The substantive classroom work happens between learners and is facilitated by a teacher.

| Activity | Primary oracy practice | Teacher role | What software does not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sleeping Dragon | shared voice awareness and group self-regulation during talk | set relative thresholds, frame the cue positively, choose preview or local listening | record speech, identify speakers, measure decibels or assess individual behaviour |
| Magic Train | observation, inference, reasoning, turn-taking and synthesis | select language level, invite contributions, probe reasons, decide when to advance | judge speech, rank learners or decide correctness |
| Listening Detectives | attentive listening, recall, evidence selection, uncertainty and retelling | read or play the case, ask for evidence, distinguish observation from inference | record audio, identify speakers or score comprehension |
| Story Machine | narrative coherence, turn-taking and building on another speaker's contribution | establish contribution rules, invite connections, introduce a twist when useful | score creativity, rewrite learner contributions or rank stories |
| Emotion Studio | vocal intention, pace, pause, stress, intonation and interpretation | give the performer a private intention and facilitate evidence-based interpretations | listen, record, detect emotion or declare one interpretation correct |
| Describe & Draw | precise description, clarification, spatial language and reflection | manage partner roles, prompt questions and guide comparison | evaluate the drawing or infer communication ability |

## Design principles

1. **Teacher judgement remains central.** The interface organises participation but does not replace professional interpretation.
2. **Reasons matter more than points.** Prompts ask learners to cite evidence, compare ideas and explain choices.
3. **Listening is visible.** Retelling a partner's contribution, asking clarification questions and revising after comparison are explicit actions.
4. **More than one answer can be legitimate.** Open prompts make uncertainty and alternative explanations discussable.
5. **No public ranking or punitive feedback.** The prototype contains no leaderboard, automatic reward, behaviour score or red/green judgement of a child.
6. **Access has fallbacks.** Listening cases can be teacher-read; activities do not depend on a remote service or high-bandwidth media.

These principles are a design rationale, not evidence of learning impact. Any outcome claim requires an appropriate study and transparent methods.
